1 · The problem

TCP blames every lost packet on traffic

TCP, the protocol behind almost everything online, treats a lost packet as proof of a traffic jam and cuts its speed in half. On a wired network that is usually right. On Wi-Fi and mobile links, many losses are just radio interference.

Think of a delivery driver who slows down every time a parcel goes missing, assuming the roads are jammed. If the parcel fell out over a pothole on an empty road, driving slower only makes every delivery late.

2 · The idea

A detective inside TCP, with seven clues

The two kinds of loss leave different fingerprints. Before a congestion loss, packets pile up in the router queue, so delays climb. A random loss arrives out of nowhere, with delays flat. At every loss, LossLens reads seven clues the sender can measure and a small decision tree reaches a verdict.

Two clues already pull the classes apart

Each dot is a real loss from the training data. Congestion losses (rose) happen when delays are up and the sender overshoots what the link carries; random losses (orange) cluster near the bottom left.

congestion lossrandom wireless loss

What happens at every loss

  1. TCP notices a packet is missing
  2. LossLens snapshots 7 clues about the recent past
  3. A depth-6 decision tree (a flowchart of yes/no questions) gives a verdict
  4. Congestion → slow down like CUBIC
    Random → keep speed and just resend
Safety first. A timeout always counts as congestion, an unsure verdict means slowing down, and the tree is tuned to catch at least 90% of real congestion losses. The worst case is today's behaviour, never worse.

6 · How we built it

Three phases, from raw simulation to a live demo

1

Simulation and data

  • ns-3.49 network: sender, router with a limited queue, Wi-Fi access point, lossy radio link, bursty background traffic
  • Every dropped packet is labelled with its true cause: queue overflow or wireless error
  • 7 sender-side clues computed at each loss
  • 300 runs over speeds, distances, loss rates, buffers and traffic
2

Classifier and LossLens TCP

  • Trained a decision tree, random forest and neural network; tested on unseen runs
  • Threshold tuned for ≥ 90% congestion recall on separate validation runs
  • Final depth-6 tree exported to plain C++ if/else
  • TcpLossAware extends CUBIC: backs off only for congestion, grows through random-loss recovery, safety fallback, oracle mode as ceiling
3

Evaluation and demo

  • 396 runs against NewReno, CUBIC, Westwood+, Veno and BBR
  • Fairness against 1 and 3 competing CUBIC users, equal start, 60 s
  • Safety test: pure congestion must match CUBIC exactly
  • This live app: real simulator runs, random networks, replay backup

7 · Honest limits, and your questions

What LossLens cannot do (yet)

Shared, full queues

When other TCP flows keep the router queue full, delays always look like congestion and LossLens falls back to plain CUBIC. It gains nothing there, but it is never worse.

Simulated radio

The wireless hop is a link with a random error model, not a full Wi-Fi model with fading and retransmissions. Real radios would be the next step.

Future work

Add a real ns-3 Wi-Fi model, richer clues for shared bottlenecks, an online-learning classifier, and a Linux kernel (eBPF) port.
